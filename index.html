CV

Hi! I am a final-year PhD candidate in Computer Science at Stanford University. I am co-advised by Dan Boneh and Dorsa Sadigh. I am also an AI Science Writer at Anthropic.

My research sits at the intersection of machine learning, safety, and security, with a focus on overcoming both the computational and cognitive barriers in verifying modern AI systems. I take an end-to-end approach to verification, including hardware-level auditing of neural networks, making model behaviors easier to evaluate, and strengthening the human expertise required to safely interact with learned policies.

My research is supported by the NSF GRFP and IBM PhD Fellowship, and has been recognized through an ICML Best Paper Runner-Up Award and the HRI Pioneers and ML Rising Stars programs. During my PhD, I have interned at Genesis Molecular AI, visited the LINGO lab at MIT, and collaborated closely with Stanford's Computation & Cognition Lab. I completed my undergrad in Computer Science and Creative Writing at Stanford, where I was a member of the Stanford NLP group and received the Ben Wegbreit Prize for Best Undergraduate Honors Thesis.

Selected Projects

  1. Optimistic verifiable training and Hawkeye, methods for auditing large neural network computations that can vary across different GPUs (NeurIPS '24, MLSys '26).
  2. One of the first controlled user studies of security vulnerabilities in AI-assisted programming (ACM CCS '23, TechCrunch).
  3. Proximal State Nudging and Z-COACH, shared-autonomy algorithms for mitigating skill atrophy and improving learning from expert AI policies using cognitive scaffolding, with Toyota (HRI '25, RA-L '26).
  4. Policy Learning with a Language Bottleneck, a method for learning interpretable RL policies that improve human-AI coordination and generalization (TMLR '26).
  5. Pyrite, a benchmark and model trained with GRPO to help chemists evaluate diffusion-predicted protein–ligand poses (Agentic AI for Biological Discovery @NeurIPS '26).
