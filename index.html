Hi! I am a PhD student in the Computer Science department at Stanford University. I am co-advised by Dorsa Sadigh and Dan Boneh. I am also an AI Science Writer at Anthropic. I am passionate about building AI systems that will positively impact human learning, and my research interests include:

  • Cognitive Modeling: How do we learn new skills over time? I study how to accurately model student learning trajectories (e.g. mistakes, skill acquisition) on platforms such as foreign language learning on Duolingo and introductory programming on Pencil Code, as well as the effects of AI assistance on problem solving.
  • Algorithmic Teaching and Discovery: As we develop black-box AI models to discover solutions to complex tasks, what algorithms can help teach humans the underlying strategies that are learned? I explore the use of skill discovery algorithms and shared autonomy for motor learning, as well as language models for feedback generation, interactive control, and cultural transmission.
  • Safety and Security: What safety and security concerns arise with modern AI systems? My work has shown that users are more likely to introduce security vulnerabilities with code generation models, and I am also excited about technical problems such as controlling GPU nondeterminism for verifiable training and mitigating bias amplification in online systems.

During my PhD, I spent time visiting MIT LINGO in Cambridge, MA. Previously, I was an AI Resident at Microsoft Research, and also worked with Hoda Heidari and Andreas Krause on algorithmic fairness through the amazing ETH Zürich Summer Research Fellowship. I completed my undergraduate studies in Computer Science and Creative Writing at Stanford, where I was a member of the Stanford NLP group. I explored robustness to distribution shifts in both machine learning (with Tatsu Hashimoto and Percy Liang) and human learning (with Kalanit Grill-Spector).
